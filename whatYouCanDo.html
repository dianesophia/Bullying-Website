You Don’t Have to Be a Bystander Who Stands By.

You may not always know exactly what to say or do when you see bullying. You do not have to confront someone aggressively or put yourself in danger. There are safe ways to make a difference.

If You’re Being Bullied

  1. Tell someone you trust. Talk to a parent, teacher, counselor, school staff member, or another trusted adult.
  2. Save evidence. For online bullying, keep screenshots, messages, usernames, dates, or other relevant information.
  3. Avoid retaliating. Responding with threats or violence can make the situation worse.
  4. Stay around supportive people. Being with trusted friends or adults can help you feel safer.
  5. Remember that it isn’t your fault. Someone else’s decision to hurt you does not define your worth.

If You Witness Bullying

You can help without putting yourself at risk.

  • Stay near the person being targeted.
  • Check on them afterward.
  • Tell a trusted adult what happened.
  • Do not join in, laugh, or encourage the behavior.
  • Report harmful online content.
  • If it is safe, use simple words such as “That’s not okay.”
  • Include people who are being left out.

If You’ve Bullied Someone

Taking responsibility is the first step toward change.

  • Stop the behavior.
  • Acknowledge the harm you caused.
  • Do not make excuses or blame the person you hurt.
  • Apologize sincerely when appropriate.
  • Ask a trusted adult for help.
  • Make an effort to change your behavior.

Everyone can choose what they do next.
