Stories & voices

Every Experience Deserves to Be Heard.

Behind every bullying statistic is a person. A student. A friend. A classmate. Someone with feelings, fears, hopes, and a story. This section gives space to different perspectives so we can understand that bullying affects more than just the person being targeted.

Stories From Students

These composite stories are based on common student experiences. Read them slowly — empathy is the skill that stops bullying earliest.

I thought nobody noticed.

I thought telling made me weak. My counselor didn’t call anyone out or make a scene. She just changed my seating, checked in every Friday, and it slowly stopped. I wish I’d told her in September instead of February.

Ayesha, 13

A student shares what it felt like to experience bullying quietly, and how speaking to someone they trusted helped them begin to feel less alone.

I Wish I Had Spoken Up Sooner.

It started as a joke edit of a photo. Forty people laughed. I laughed too, because the alternative was being next. When I finally left the chat, three others messaged me privately saying they hated it. We were all waiting for someone else to go first.

Dan, 16

A bystander reflects on watching someone being excluded and learning that staying silent can sometimes make a person feel even more alone.

I Realized I Was Part of the Problem.

I called it banter. He didn’t. When our teacher made me actually listen to how his week felt, I couldn’t unhear it. Apologising was the most uncomfortable ten seconds of my life and the best thing I’ve done at that school.

Jonas, 15

A student discusses recognizing harmful behavior in themselves and choosing to take responsibility and change.

Being excluded

Nobody hit me. They just stopped saving me a seat. For two months I ate lunch in the library and told my mum I liked the quiet. One girl finally asked why I never came to the canteen — and then walked there with me. That was it. That was the whole fix.

Mira, 14

Exclusion is bullying too. An invitation costs nothing.
