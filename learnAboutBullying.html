Learn About Bullying

What Is Bullying?

Bullying is repeated harmful behavior that involves an imbalance of power. It can be physical, verbal, social, or online, and it can happen in places where people should feel safe, including schools, communities, and social media.

Bullying is not simply “a joke” when someone is being hurt by it.

Types of Bullying

Physical Bullying

Hitting, pushing, kicking, damaging someone’s belongings, or using physical actions to intimidate another person.

Verbal Bullying

Insults, threats, name-calling, mocking, humiliating comments, or repeatedly making hurtful remarks.

Social Bullying

Deliberately excluding someone, spreading rumors, embarrassing someone, or encouraging others to turn against them.

Cyberbullying

Using phones, social media, messaging apps, games, or other online platforms to threaten, harass, embarrass, or hurt someone.

What Bullying Can Look Like

  • Repeatedly making someone the target of jokes
  • Spreading rumors about someone
  • Purposely excluding someone from a group
  • Threatening or intimidating another person
  • Posting embarrassing content about someone
  • Sending repeated hurtful messages
  • Encouraging others to gang up on someone

Bullying vs. Conflict

Not every disagreement is bullying. People can argue, misunderstand each other, or have conflicts without one person repeatedly targeting another.

What matters is understanding the situation, recognizing harmful behavior, and making sure people are treated with dignity and respect.

Why Does It Matter?

Bullying can affect a person’s confidence, relationships, sense of safety, school experience, and emotional well-being. Even when someone says they are “just joking,” the impact on another person can still be serious.

Bottom line: If someone is being hurt, humiliated, threatened, or repeatedly targeted, it deserves attention.
